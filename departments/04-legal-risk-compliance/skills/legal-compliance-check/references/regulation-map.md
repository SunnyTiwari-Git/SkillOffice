# Regulation map: starting points

A short orientation to rules that often come up. It is not complete and not legal advice. Laws, rules and guidance change; always confirm current requirements with counsel and official sources for the jurisdictions involved.

## Data protection and privacy

| Rule | Where it bites | Themes to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GDPR (EU) and UK GDPR | Personal data of people in the EU, EEA or UK, including by organizations outside those areas that offer them goods or services or monitor them | Lawful basis for each use; transparency notices; data subject rights; impact assessments for high-risk processing; records of processing; processor contracts; security; breach notification to the authority (commonly cited as within 72 hours of becoming aware, where notification is required); transfers outside the area |
| India Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) | Digital personal data processed in India, and processing outside India connected with offering goods or services to people in India | Notice and consent (or another permitted ground); rights of data principals; duties of data fiduciaries, including security and breach reporting; children's data; significant data fiduciary duties. Check the rules notified under the Act and their commencement dates with counsel |
| CCPA as amended by CPRA (California) | Businesses meeting the statute's thresholds that handle California residents' personal information | Notice at collection; rights to know, delete, correct and opt out of sale or sharing; limits on sensitive personal information; service provider contract terms |
| Other national privacy laws | For example Brazil (LGPD), Canada, Singapore, Australia, China (PIPL) | Similar themes with local differences, especially on cross-border transfers and localization |

## Sector rules and standards

| Rule | Where it bites | Themes to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HIPAA (US) | Protected health information handled by covered entities and their business associates | Business associate agreements, safeguards, minimum necessary use, breach notification rules |
| PCI DSS | Anyone who stores, processes or transmits payment card data (a contractual industry standard, not a law) | Scope reduction (for example hosted payment pages), never storing sensitive authentication data, access control, testing |
| Marketing and messaging rules | Email, SMS and calls to consumers (for example CAN-SPAM, TCPA in the US, e-privacy rules in Europe, telecom rules in India) | Consent, opt-out, sender identification, quiet hours |
| Consumer protection | Advertising claims, subscriptions and cancellation, pricing display | Substantiation of claims, clear terms, easy cancellation |

## Security and assurance frameworks (voluntary, often required by customers)

| Framework | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| SOC 2 | Controls at service organizations across security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy |
| ISO/IEC 27001 | An information security management system: risk assessment, controls, continual improvement |

## Questions that usually decide the answer

1. Whose data, and where do those people live?
2. What kind of data (ordinary, sensitive, health, financial, children's, biometric)?
3. Where is it stored and who processes it (vendors, other countries)?
4. On what basis is it collected (consent, contract, legal duty, other)?
5. How long is it kept and how is it deleted?
6. What do customers' contracts and the company's own policies already promise?
