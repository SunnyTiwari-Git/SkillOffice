# Policy and notice outlines

Starting structures. Adapt them to the company and to what counsel requires in each jurisdiction.

## Privacy notice (public)

1. Who we are and how to contact us (and any privacy or data protection officer or local representative).
2. What personal data we collect, by source (from you, automatically, from others).
3. Why we use it, and the legal ground for each use where the law requires one.
4. Who we share it with (categories of vendors and partners) and whether any sharing counts as a sale or sharing under local law.
5. International transfers and how they are protected.
6. How long we keep it.
7. How we protect it.
8. Your rights and how to use them, including complaints to the authority.
9. Children.
10. Cookies and similar technologies (or a link to the cookie notice).
11. Changes to this notice and the effective date.

## Cookie notice

What cookies and similar technologies are used, grouped by purpose (strictly necessary, preferences, analytics, advertising); who sets them; how long they last; how to accept, refuse or change choices; and whether non-essential cookies wait for consent where the law requires it.

## Terms of service outline

Who may use the service; accounts; acceptable use (or link); fees, billing and renewal; customer content and licences; intellectual property; third-party services; availability and changes; disclaimers; limitation of liability; indemnity; suspension and termination; governing law and disputes; changes to the terms; contact. Mark every risk allocation term `[COUNSEL]`.

## Acceptable use policy

Scope (who and which systems); permitted use; prohibited conduct; security duties (passwords, devices, reporting incidents); monitoring notice where lawful; consequences; how to report concerns.

## Data retention and legal hold policy

Purpose and scope; retention schedule (record type, owner, period, trigger, deletion method); legal hold process that suspends deletion when litigation or an investigation is reasonably expected; secure deletion; exceptions and approvals; review cycle.

## Whistleblowing (speak-up) policy

What can be reported; how to report (including anonymously where lawful); protection from retaliation; how reports are handled and who sees them; timelines for acknowledgment; record keeping; external reporting routes where the law provides them.

## Anti-bribery and gifts policy

Prohibited payments; gifts and hospitality limits and approvals; dealing with public officials; third parties acting for the company; record keeping; reporting concerns.

## Vendor code of conduct

Legal compliance; labour and human rights; health and safety; environment; anti-corruption; data protection and security; conflicts of interest; audit and reporting; consequences.
