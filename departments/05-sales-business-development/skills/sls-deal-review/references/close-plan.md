# Close plan drafts

## Business case

Written in the customer's language and grounded in their own words. Each point cites the call or email it came from.

1. **Current state and its cost**: the problem as they described it.
2. **Desired outcome**: their success measures and what drives their timeline.
3. **Proposed solution**: what they would buy, mapped to each outcome.
4. **Investment and return**: price against the value they quantified, with simple math. Prices are placeholders, "[to be confirmed by the team]", until the team approves them.
5. **Risk of waiting**: what delay costs, in their terms.
6. **Why us**: only differentiators they have actually reacted to.

Flag every claim that has no customer evidence behind it. Those are points to validate on the next call, not to assert in the document.

## Mutual action plan

Work backward from the target signature date through both sides' remaining steps: validation, security review, legal review, procurement, internal approvals and signatures, each with realistic turnaround.

| Step | Owner (us, customer or a named role) | Target date | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Flag any step whose date makes the target signature date impossible, and give the earliest credible date instead.

## Before anything is shared

The business case and the plan are drafts for the seller and their team. Pricing, discounts, contract terms and delivery dates need approval from the team before the customer sees them.
