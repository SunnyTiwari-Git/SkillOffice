# Asset structures

## Pitch deck (10 to 12 slides)

1. The problem the buyer lives with today.
2. What the problem costs (time, money, risk).
3. The shift creating urgency now.
4. The company's approach.
5. Three or four key workflows, not a feature tour.
6. Proof points.
7. One customer story told well.
8. How they get from here to live.
9. Expected return and payback period.
10. Pricing overview (placeholders until the team approves).
11. Next steps with a timeline.

One idea per slide. Design for presenting, not reading.

| Buyer | Emphasize | Play down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical buyer | Architecture, security, integrations | Business metrics |
| Economic buyer | Return, payback, total cost, risk | Technical detail |
| Champion | Internal selling points, quick wins, peer proof | Deep technical or financial detail |

## One-pager

Problem in one sentence; the solution; three differentiators; one proof point; a clear next step with a named contact. One page, scannable in 30 seconds.

## Objection document

Categories: price, timing, competition, authority, status quo, technical. For each objection: how sellers hear it, the real concern, the response, one proof point, a follow-up question. Provide a one-screen quick-reference table plus a longer version for training.

## Demo script

Opening and agenda (2 minutes); recap of what was learned in discovery (3 minutes); 3 or 4 workflows mapped to their pain (15 to 20 minutes); questions to ask during the demo; summary and next steps with a timeline (5 minutes). Demo after discovery, never before. Use their terms.

## Cold call script

A permission-based opener, the reason for calling tied to a specific observation, one question about their situation, one-line answers to the first pushback ("not interested", "send me an email", "we already use something"), and a 20-second voicemail. Respect do-not-call rules where they apply.

## ROI calculator

Inputs the prospect provides (time on manual work, current tool costs, error rates, team size); formulas (time saved, cost reduced, revenue impact); outputs (annual return, payback months, three-year value). Sample data is labeled as sample data.

## Case study brief

Customer profile (industry, size, buyer role); challenge in 2 to 3 sentences; solution in 1 to 2 sentences; 3 before-and-after results; one quote; tags for industry, use case, size and persona. Needs the customer's permission before external use.

## Proposal template

Executive summary (one page); proposed solution mapped to their requirements; implementation plan with milestones and responsibilities; investment section with pricing placeholders and terms marked for approval; next steps and decision timeline. Aim for 5 to 7 pages and mirror their language from discovery.

## Sales playbook

Buyer profile; qualification criteria; discovery questions by topic; top 10 objections; competitive positioning; demo flow per persona; email templates (follow-up, proposal, check-in, close-the-loop). Review quarterly, with a named owner.

## Persona card

Role and title; goals; pains; top 3 to 5 objections; evaluation criteria; role in the buying process; the one sentence that resonates most. Persona types: economic buyer, technical buyer, end user, champion, blocker.
