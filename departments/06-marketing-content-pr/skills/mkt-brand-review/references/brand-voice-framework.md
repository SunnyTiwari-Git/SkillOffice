# Brand voice framework

Use this to evaluate content, or to help the user document their voice into the context profile (`brand.voice`, `brand.tone_dos`, `brand.tone_donts`).

## What a brand voice document covers

1. Personality: the brand described as a person.
2. Voice attributes: 3-5, each with what it means, what it does not mean, and an example.
3. Audience: who the brand speaks to, their expertise, how they expect to be addressed.
4. Messaging pillars: 3-5 themes and how each connects to audience needs.
5. Tone range: how the voice adapts by context.
6. Style rules: grammar, formatting, numbers and dates.
7. Terminology: preferred and avoided terms.

## Voice attribute spectrums

| Spectrum | One end | Other end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formality | Formal | Conversational |
| Authority | Expert | Peer |
| Emotion | Warm | Matter-of-fact |
| Complexity | Technical | Simple |
| Energy | Bold | Calm |
| Humor | Playful | Serious |

Attribute format:

- **We are**: what it means in practice.
- **We are not**: the common misreading.
- **Sounds like**: an example sentence.
- **Does not sound like**: a counter-example.

## Tone by channel

| Channel | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Blog | Informative, conversational |
| LinkedIn | Professional, concise |
| X (Twitter) | Direct, sometimes witty |
| Email | Personal, helpful, action-oriented |
| Sales material | Confident, specific, benefit-led |
| Help docs | Clear, patient, step-by-step |
| Press release | Formal, factual |
| Error messages | Empathetic, blame-free |

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Launch | Confident, forward-looking |
| Incident or outage | Transparent, accountable, empathetic |
| Customer story | Celebratory, crediting the customer |
| Bad news (price rise, removal of a feature) | Honest, respectful, solution-focused |
| Competitor comparison | Fair, factual, never disparaging |

The voice attributes stay fixed; tone dials them up or down by context.

## Style decisions to record

Serial comma yes or no; sentence or title case for headings; contractions; numbers spelled out or numerals; percent sign or word; date and time format (from regional settings); punctuation on list items; exclamation mark policy; emoji by channel.

## Terminology table

| log in (verb) | login (verb) | "login" is the noun |
| set up (verb) | setup (verb) | "setup" is the noun |
| email | e-mail | |

Add the company's product names with exact capitalization, and how to refer to competitors and the product category.

## Inclusive language

Use gender-neutral language for unknown people; avoid ableist idioms; prefer "straightforward" to "easy"; avoid idioms that do not translate across regions.
