# PR playbook

## The PR mix

| Mode | What it is | Effort | Speed to coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reactive (newsjacking) | Add the company's informed view to a trending story | Low to medium | Hours to days |
| Proactive (pitching) | Build a target list and pitch original stories | High | 2-8 weeks |
| Inbound (reporter requests) | Answer journalist queries on request services | Low | Days to weeks |
| Owned (press page, media kit) | Make it easy for journalists to find facts and assets | One-time setup | Ongoing |

Speed matters for reactive PR: a good pitch in the first hour of a story beats a perfect pitch days later.

## Story angles

- **Founding story**: why the company exists, told through a specific moment.
- **Small player, big shift**: a smaller company doing something established players do not.
- **A broken system**: the company fights a problem everyone recognizes (a process, a cost, an outdated norm). Never frame a named competitor as the enemy.
- **Data story**: original or aggregated data with one clear finding.

A milestone alone is not a story. A milestone with a narrative can be.

## When to wait

- Before launch with no story beyond "we exist".
- No one can sustain pitching for 4-6 weeks.
- No clear audience: journalists ask who reads their piece because of this.

## Pitch quality bar (all must be yes before sending)

- [ ] The journalist covers this beat (checked their recent articles).
- [ ] There is a clear news hook, something that just happened or is about to.
- [ ] The journalist could write the story from this email alone (data, quote, customer, contact).
- [ ] The subject line predicts the headline.
- [ ] Under 150 words.
- [ ] No hype words.
- [ ] The ask is clear (interview, exclusive, embargo, quote).

## Press page and media kit

- One-paragraph company description, ready to copy.
- Leadership bios and high-resolution headshots (with consent).
- Logo pack (vector and image formats, light and dark versions, usage rules).
- Product screenshots.
- Recent coverage list.
- Founding date, team size, funding if disclosed.
- A press contact email (not only a form) and a response promise the team can keep.

## Crisis response

### Severity

| Level | Example | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Minor | A few public complaints | Reply on the same channel, fix, log |
| 2 Moderate | Visible outage, growing complaints | Status update, holding statement, regular updates |
| 3 Severe | Data incident, safety issue, legal or regulatory matter, executive controversy | Leadership and legal counsel involved before any statement; one spokesperson |

### First 60 minutes

1. Confirm the facts. Separate what is known from what is assumed.
2. Name one spokesperson.
3. Publish a holding statement on owned channels.
4. Set and keep the time of the next update.
5. Brief customer-facing staff with the same facts.

### Holding statement template

"We are aware of {issue} affecting {who}. Our team is {action being taken}. We will share an update by {time and date}. {Where to get help or more information}."

### Apology template

"{What happened, in plain words}. This affected {who and how}. We are sorry. Here is what we have done: {actions}. Here is what we are changing so it does not happen again: {changes}. {How to contact us}."

All crisis statements are drafts until reviewed by leadership and, for severity 3, by legal counsel.
