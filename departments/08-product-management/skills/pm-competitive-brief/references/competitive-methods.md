# Competitive analysis methods

## Competitive set

| Level | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | Same problem, same users, same approach | Products customers evaluate against you |
| Indirect | Same problem, different approach | Spreadsheets instead of a dedicated tool |
| Adjacent | Not competing today, could expand into your space | A platform that could add your feature |
| Substitute | Entirely different way to meet the need | Hiring a person, outsourcing, doing nothing |

Landscape axes that often reveal positioning: suite versus point solution, small business versus enterprise, self-serve versus sales-led, simple versus powerful, horizontal versus vertical.

## Feature comparison

Rating scale (default): Strong (market-leading), Adequate (works, not differentiated), Weak (exists with significant gaps), Absent.
For deep dives, a 0 to 5 scale: 5 best-in-class, 4 strong, 3 adequate, 2 limited, 1 minimal, 0 absent.

```
| Capability area | Us | Competitor A | Competitor B | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Area] |  |  |  |  |
|   [Capability] | Strong | Adequate | Absent | [buyer impact] |
```

Rate from product experience, customer feedback and reviews, not marketing claims. A comparison where you always win is not credible.

## Positioning

Template: For [target customer] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [alternative], [product] [differentiator].

Message levels: category claim, differentiator, value proposition, proof points.

Look for unclaimed positions (valuable and owned by no one), crowded positions (claims everyone makes), emerging positions (driven by market change) and vulnerable positions (claims a competitor cannot fully deliver).

## Win/loss input

- Best evidence: customer interviews soon after the decision; CRM notes are faster but biased.
- Separate product reasons (features, quality) from non-product reasons (price model, brand, relationship, timing, integrations, incumbent switching cost).
- Track the top 3 to 5 reasons over time and win rate by competitor.

## Trends

For each trend: what is changing, why now, who is affected, timeline, implication for us, what competitors are doing.
Separate signal (behavior data, investment, regulation, customer demand) from noise (hype only). Test trends against your own customers' data.
Responses: lead, fast follow, monitor with a trigger, or ignore with a documented reason.

## Pricing caveats

Packaging differs (per seat, usage-based, custom enterprise pricing). Show prices in their original currency and note the date checked.
