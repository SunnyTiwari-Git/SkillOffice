# Product metrics guide

## Hierarchy

- **North Star**: the single metric that best captures value delivered. It moves when users get more value, predicts long-term success, can be influenced by the team and is easy to understand. Examples: weekly active teams with 3 or more contributors (collaboration tool), weekly completed transactions (marketplace), weekly users completing the core workflow (business software).
- **Health metrics (5 to 7)** across the lifecycle:
  - Acquisition: signups, signup conversion, channel mix, cost per acquisition.
  - Activation: activation rate (the key action that predicts retention), time to activate, setup completion.
  - Engagement: daily, weekly and monthly active users, stickiness (daily over monthly actives), core action frequency, feature adoption.
  - Retention: day 1, 7 and 30 retention, cohort curves, churn, resurrection.
  - Monetization: free to paid conversion, recurring revenue, revenue per account, expansion, net revenue retention.
  - Satisfaction: NPS, CSAT, support volume and resolution time, review sentiment.
- **Diagnostic metrics**: funnel steps, feature usage, segment breakdowns, performance (load time, error rate, latency).

## Reading common metrics

- Active users: define "active" carefully (a core action, not a login). Trend matters more than the absolute number. Stickiness above 0.5 suggests daily habit; below 0.2 suggests infrequent use.
- Retention: plot cohort curves. An early drop suggests an activation problem; steady decline an engagement problem; flattening means a stable retained base. Newer cohorts retaining better means improvements work.
- Conversion: map the funnel, find the biggest drop-off, segment by source and user type.
- Activation: choose the action that best separates retained from churned users and is reachable early.

## Targets and goals

- Targets need a baseline, a benchmark or trajectory, and a commit and a stretch level.
- OKRs: 2 or 3 objectives with 2 to 4 measurable, outcome-based key results each; about 70 percent completion is normal for stretch OKRs; grade honestly.

## Review cadences

- Weekly (15 to 30 minutes): North Star, notable health moves, experiments, anomalies.
- Monthly (30 to 60 minutes): full scorecard, progress to goals, cohorts, launches, segments; pick 1 to 3 actions.
- Quarterly: goal scoring, quarter trends, year-over-year, strategy adjustments, next goals.

## Dashboards

Start from the decision it supports; show the most important metric most prominently; always show comparison and trend; 5 to 10 metrics, not 50; one time period per view; every metric actionable.
Avoid vanity metrics (all-time totals), output metrics (tickets closed), stale dashboards and one view for every audience.

## Alerts

Threshold, sustained-trend and anomaly alerts; every alert actionable, owned, tuned and given a severity.
