# Roadmap methods

## Formats

| Format | Best for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Now / Next / Later | Most teams, leadership and external audiences | Now = committed, high confidence. Next = 1 to 3 months, planned. Later = 3 to 6+ months, directional. Avoids false date precision. |
| Quarterly themes | Showing strategic alignment | 2 or 3 themes per quarter mapped to goals, initiatives under each. |
| OKR-aligned | Teams that run on OKRs | Initiatives listed under the key result they should move, with expected impact. |
| Timeline (Gantt) | Execution planning with engineering | Shows sequencing and conflicts. Not for external audiences. |

## Prioritization methods

- **RICE** = (Reach x Impact x Confidence) / Effort. Reach in users per period; Impact 3, 2, 1, 0.5 or 0.25; Confidence 100%, 80% or 50%; Effort in person-months. Good for large backlogs; weak for strategic bets.
- **ICE** = Impact x Confidence x Ease, each 1 to 10. Quick, for early-stage products.
- **MoSCoW**: must, should, could, won't this time. Good for scoping a release or quarter.
- **Value versus effort**: quick wins (do first), big bets (plan carefully), fill-ins (spare capacity), money pits (remove).

Show the scores and assumptions so the ranking can be challenged.

## Dependencies

Types: technical, team, external (vendor or partner), knowledge (research needed first), sequential.
For each: owner, need-by date, buffer, contingency if it slips.
To reduce them: a simpler version that avoids the dependency, an interface contract or mock to work in parallel, resequencing, or absorbing the work.

## Capacity

- Start from people and time; subtract meetings, on-call, interviews, holidays and leave.
- A common split: about 70% planned features, 20% technical health, 10% unplanned. Adjust for context (new product: more features; post-incident: more reliability).
- If commitments exceed capacity, cut scope or move items. Fewer, reliable commitments beat over-commitment.

## Communicating changes

1. State what changed. 2. Explain the new information behind it. 3. Show the trade-off. 4. Show the new plan. 5. Tell the people affected directly.
Avoid roadmap whiplash: batch updates at a regular cadence unless urgent, and distinguish strategic reprioritization from normal scope adjustment.
