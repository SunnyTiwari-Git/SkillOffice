# Research methods

Rough starting points, not rules. Adjust to the question, budget and audience.

| Method | Best for | Typical sample | Typical time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| User interviews | Needs, motivations, current workflow | 5 to 8 per segment | 2 to 4 weeks |
| Usability testing | Whether a specific design or flow works | 5 to 8 | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Surveys | How common an attitude or behavior is | 100 or more | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Card sorting | How users group content (information architecture) | 15 to 30 | About 1 week |
| Tree testing | Whether users can find things in a proposed structure | 30 or more | About 1 week |
| Diary studies | Behavior over days or weeks | 10 to 15 | 2 to 8 weeks |
| A/B testing | Comparing two live designs | Enough for statistical significance | 1 to 4 weeks |

## Interview guide structure

1. Warm-up (about 5 minutes): introductions, consent, explain there are no wrong answers.
2. Context (about 10 minutes): how they do the task today.
3. Deep dive (about 20 minutes): the specific topic. Ask for stories ("Tell me about the last time..."), not opinions.
4. Reaction (about 10 minutes): concepts or prototypes, if any.
5. Wrap-up (about 5 minutes): anything missed, thanks, next steps.

## Analysis frameworks

- Affinity mapping: group observations into themes.
- Impact and effort matrix: prioritize opportunities.
- Journey mapping: steps, feelings and pain points over time.
- Jobs to be done: what the user is trying to get done, in their words.

## Good practice

- Ask about past behavior, not predictions ("Would you use...?" is unreliable).
- Avoid leading questions ("Don't you find this confusing?").
- Record only with consent, and store recordings outside SkillOffice memory.
