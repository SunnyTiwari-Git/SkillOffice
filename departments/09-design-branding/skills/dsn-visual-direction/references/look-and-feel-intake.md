# Look-and-feel intake

Ask these before any new visual design, unless the brief or `brand.look_and_feel` already answers them. Ask in one go, as multiple choice with a free-text "other", and skip any question already answered. Six questions at most.

Words like "modern", "clean", "premium", "friendly" or "minimal" mean different things to different people. Never design from them alone: turn them into the concrete choices below.

## The questions

1. **Style.** Which feels closest? Offer 3 or 4 options that fit the subject, each described by what the reader would see, for example:
   - Fashion editorial: huge type, sharp edges, lots of white space, no card boxes.
   - Clean product: calm colors, simple grid, clear screenshots or product images.
   - Handmade and warm: textures, softer type, natural colors, a personal voice.
   - Bold and graphic: strong color blocks, big shapes, high contrast.
2. **Experience.** What should visiting the page feel like?
   - A quick, scannable page.
   - A story that unfolds as you scroll.
   - An interactive tool (a builder, estimator or configurator).
3. **Motion.** How much movement?
   - None.
   - Subtle: gentle fades and hover effects.
   - Scroll-driven storytelling: pictures that change as you scroll.
4. **Imagery.** What pictures do we have or want?
   - Real photos (ask who will supply them).
   - Illustration.
   - Generated textures or abstract visuals.
   - Product screenshots or renders.
5. **Density and tone.** Airy and calm, balanced, or dense and information-rich? Playful or serious?
6. **References.** One to three websites they like and one they dislike, and what they like or dislike about each (the type, the colors, the motion, the layout). Borrow principles from these, never identity.

## After the answers

1. Offer **2 or 3 contrasting directions**, each in under 60 words: the feel, the palette in words, the type pairing in words, the layout idea and the one bold element. Make them clearly different from each other.
2. Build only the direction the user picks.
3. Propose saving the answers and the chosen direction as `brand.look_and_feel` values, following the context protocol of the `skilloffice-core` skill. Save only after the user confirms. Next time, read them and ask only what has changed.

## Example of turning a vague word into choices

The user says "warm and friendly". Ask: "Warm like a handmade studio (textures, natural colors), warm like a family brand (rounded type, soft colors), or warm like a boutique (editorial type with warm accent colors)?"
